## Description
With funding from the SMFA Dean’s Research Fund, this research project aims to explore spontaneous drawing, which is a state embedded in traditional Chinese painting. The project is inspired by the observation of spontaneity in nature and the harmonious result of such creation. Through exploring spontaneous creation in the scope of traditional Chinese painting, Yuchan adapts her observations to a more aesthetic way of drawing landscapes. While creating a set of work, she contemplated the relationship between the spontaneous creation process, nature’s course, and life philosophies that is applicable to every individual.

## Laying the Foundation of Chinese Ink Painting: 
Shanghai and Kyoto
During the summer break, Yuchan took classes in Chinese Ink Painting with Master Cui Wen Huan in Shanghai. Through copying work of  Pu Ru (溥儒) and Lan Ying (蓝瑛), Yuchan explored Cun Fa (皴法, the brush techniques used for painting mountains), the relationship between Xu (虚, emptiness) and Shi (实, solidity) in composition, and refined her approach to color application. She visited Kyoto City Kyocera Museum of Art, Kyoto National Museum, and China Art Museum where she saw work of both traditional paintings and contemporary arts.

## Exploration of Composition, Abstraction, and Styles:
Boston
During the fall semester in Boston, through Independent Studies with Professor Ethan Murrow from the SMFA, Yuchan explored composition, abstraction, and styles in her own creation. Through this valuable experience, she stepped outside the habit of drawing things as they are, which she acquired through her over ten years of sketching experience.

## Final Project:
Boston
In the end of the half-year project, Yuchan found most joy and inspiration in the spontaneous drawing of trees with pencils. The simplicity of media and the emphasis of lines are perfect to stress the trivial uniqueness of each stroke as they appear on the paper without planning or modification. Nature itself is a collection of spontaneous and random phenomena. Yuchan connects this to each individual’s experience in life: each person could be seen as the drawing, and each of their behavior is seen as the stroke. To visualize the idea, she created a scroll of trees where none of the lines are planned or modified.
